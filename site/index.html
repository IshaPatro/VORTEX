01 — Overview

What VORTEX does

Every investor holding a portfolio of assets faces two recurring questions: how much risk am I carrying right now, and how would my holdings withstand a severe market event. Answering them rigorously normally demands specialist tooling and fluency in quantitative finance.

VORTEX automates the entire workflow. It ingests two decades of market history, identifies the prevailing market environment, quantifies downside risk through multiple methodologies, simulates historical crises against the live portfolio, and — distinctively — uses a language model to translate the resulting figures into clear, actionable commentary.

In plain terms

Think of VORTEX as an instrument panel for a portfolio. The gauges are risk metrics, the indicators are market regimes and stress tests, and the commentary layer is the analyst who tells you what the readings actually mean for your decisions.

02 — Foundations

Two concepts to anchor everything

Nearly every metric in this reference is built on these two ideas.

Concept 01

Return

A return is the percentage change in an asset's value over a period. A move from $100 to $105 is a +5% return. VORTEX works with daily returns — the granular day-to-day fluctuations that reveal an asset's behaviour.

Concept 02

Volatility

Volatility measures the magnitude of those fluctuations. A stable instrument barely moves; a speculative one swings sharply. Higher volatility signals greater uncertainty and, generally, greater risk — it is the raw material for most risk metrics.

03 — The Metrics

Risk metrics, defined precisely and plainly

These are the core gauges on the dashboard. Each answers a distinct question about a portfolio's exposure. The formal definitions for each appear later in the Methodology section.

Value-at-Risk (VaR)

What constitutes a realistic bad day?

VaR estimates the loss that should not be exceeded on a typical adverse day, at a chosen confidence level. A 95% daily VaR of -2% indicates that on 95% of days the loss is expected to remain within 2%; on the remaining ~1 day in 20, losses may be larger. It is the industry-standard single-number summary of downside exposure.

Conditional VaR (CVaR / Expected Shortfall)

And how severe are the worst days?

Where VaR marks the threshold of the loss tail, CVaR measures the average loss once that threshold is breached. It captures the depth of tail risk that VaR alone omits, and is especially informative for portfolios exposed to rare but severe events.

Annualized Volatility

How variable are returns over a year?

Daily volatility scaled to an annual figure for comparability. A broad equity index sits near 15%; concentrated single names or digital assets can reach 30–60% or more. Higher values denote a wider range of plausible outcomes.

Sharpe Ratio

Is the return adequate for the risk taken?

Return earned per unit of volatility. It quantifies risk-adjusted performance: values above 1.0 are considered solid, and above 2.0 excellent. A low ratio indicates substantial risk for limited compensation.

Maximum Drawdown

What was the deepest decline endured?

The largest peak-to-trough fall over the period. A -37% drawdown means the portfolio was, at its worst, 37% below its prior high. It is the most tangible measure of the loss an investor would have had to tolerate before recovery.

Skewness

Do surprises tend to be positive or negative?

The asymmetry of the return distribution. Negative skew, common in markets, reflects frequent small gains punctuated by occasional large losses — gradual climbs and abrupt declines. Recognising it in advance is valuable for risk planning.

Kurtosis (tail risk)

How frequent are extreme outcomes?

The propensity for extreme results. High kurtosis — "fat tails" — means severe moves occur more often than a normal distribution predicts. Markets exhibit fat tails, which is precisely why crisis-scale events recur more frequently than naive models imply.

Correlation

Do holdings move together?

The degree to which two assets move in tandem, from +1 (lockstep) to 0 (independent) to -1 (opposite). Effective diversification depends on holding assets that do not decline simultaneously; VORTEX renders a correlation matrix to surface hidden concentration.

04 — Market Regimes

Identifying the prevailing market environment

Markets behave differently across distinct environments. VORTEX classifies the current regime automatically using a Hidden Markov Model.

The method, in plain terms

A Hidden Markov Model (HMM) infers an unobservable state from observable evidence. It never sees the market's "environment" directly, but by analysing the pattern of daily returns and volatility it deduces the hidden state driving them — and detects when that state transitions. The patterns are learned without supervision; no labels are provided in advance.

Low Volatility

Calm, steadily appreciating markets — the low-risk, high-confidence environment.

Crisis

Sharp declines and elevated volatility — the 2008 and COVID-crash environment.

Recovery

Returns turn positive and volatility subsides following a shock.

Inflation Shock

Rising-price pressure, where equities and bonds can decline together.

On the dashboard, these regimes are overlaid as coloured bands behind the price series, making the timing of every environment shift over the past two decades immediately legible.

05 — Value-at-Risk

Four methodologies, compared

There is no single correct way to estimate risk, so VORTEX computes four and presents them side by side. Each rests on a different assumption about how markets behave.

Historical VaR

Reads the loss threshold directly from the empirical distribution of past returns. Assumption-light and transparent, but presumes the future resembles the observed past.

Parametric VaR

Fits a normal distribution to returns and derives the threshold analytically. Fast and smooth, though it tends to understate the likelihood of extreme events.

EWMA VaR (RiskMetrics)

Weights recent observations more heavily, so the estimate responds quickly as conditions change. Well-suited to detecting emerging turbulence early.

Regime-Adaptive VaR VORTEX signature

Conditions the estimate on the detected regime: the risk band widens during a Crisis and tightens in calm periods. Risk that adapts to context — as a discretionary risk manager would.

06 — Volatility Forecasting

Anticipating future turbulence

Measuring current risk is necessary; estimating future risk is more valuable. VORTEX applies three complementary techniques.

Statistical

Rolling Volatility

A moving average of recent variability — the baseline measure of how turbulent conditions have been.

Weighted

EWMA

The same principle with exponential decay, so the estimate adapts faster to fresh market shocks.

Machine Learning

XGBoost

A gradient-boosted model trained on lagged return and volatility features to forecast forward realised volatility — capturing non-linear relationships simpler averages miss.

07 — Stress Testing

Simulating historical crises

A stress test applies a known historical shock to the live portfolio and reports the resulting loss — quantifying exposure before a comparable event occurs.

2008 Financial Crisis

The subprime-driven collapse; equities lost roughly half their value and the banking system seized.

COVID-19 Crash (2020)

The fastest bear market on record — a 34% decline in 33 days.

Fed Rate Shock (2022)

A rare episode in which equities and bonds fell together as interest rates rose sharply.

Tech Selloff (Q1 2022)

Long-duration technology names compressed as the era of cheap capital ended.

Each scenario applies calibrated, asset-specific shocks (gold, for instance, often rises while equities fall) and reports the aggregate impact on the portfolio — isolating its single largest vulnerability.

09 — Quantitative Methodology

The mathematics behind every panel

Formal definitions for each component of the engine, for readers who want the underlying equations.

Returns

Daily log-returns are used throughout for time-additivity:

rt = ln( Pt / Pt−1 )

The portfolio return is the weight-normalized combination rp,t = Σi wi ri,t, computed only on dates where every holding traded.

Regime Detection — Gaussian HMM

A four-state Gaussian Hidden Markov Model is fit on the feature vector [ rt, σt(5) ] (return and 5-day realized volatility):

P(st | st−1) = A  ;  xt | st ~ 𝒩(μs, Σs)

States are labelled post-hoc by their mean return and volatility into Low-Volatility, Crisis, Recovery and Inflation Shock.

Historical VaR

The empirical quantile of the trailing return window (non-parametric):

VaRα = Quantile(1−α)( {rt−w…t} )

Parametric VaR

Assumes Gaussian returns and uses the normal inverse-CDF z-score:

VaRα = μ + z(1−α) · σ

EWMA VaR — RiskMetrics

Exponentially-weighted variance reacts faster to recent shocks (λ = 0.94):

σt2 = λ·σt−12 + (1−λ)·rt−12
VaRα = z(1−α) · σt

Regime-Adaptive VaR signature

Conditions the empirical quantile on the HMM state, widening in a Crisis and tightening when calm. It is strictly causal — each day uses only past returns from the same regime (no look-ahead):

VaRα(t) = Quantile(1−α)( r<t | regime = st )

Cornish-Fisher VaR

Adjusts the Gaussian quantile for the sample skewness (S) and excess kurtosis (K) — far more honest for fat-tailed assets:

zcf = z + (z²−1)·S/6 + (z³−3z)·K/24 − (2z³−5z)·S²/36

Conditional VaR (Expected Shortfall)

The mean loss conditional on breaching VaR — capturing tail depth:

CVaRα = E[ r | r ≤ VaRα ]

Volatility Forecasting

Three lenses, annualized by √252: rolling std, EWMA, and a gradient-boosted (XGBoost) model. The XGBoost forecast is walk-forward (expanding window, periodic refit) and scored out-of-sample against a naive benchmark via RMSE and QLIKE.

σann = σdaily · √252

Sharpe Ratio

Annualized risk-adjusted return (rf = 0):

S = ( E[r] / σr ) · √252

Maximum Drawdown

Deepest peak-to-trough decline of cumulative wealth Wt:

MDD = mint ( Wt / maxτ≤t Wτ − 1 )

Stress Testing

Each scenario applies calibrated per-asset shocks δi to the portfolio weights:

Lscenario = Σi wi · δi

Higher Moments

Skewness (asymmetry) and excess kurtosis (tail fatness) flag the non-normal, fat-tailed risk that VaR alone understates:

γ1 = E[(r−μ)3]/σ3  ;  γ2 = E[(r−μ)4]/σ4 − 3

Sortino Ratio

Like Sharpe, but penalizes only downside deviation σd — a fairer reward-to-risk measure when returns are asymmetric:

Sortino = ( E[r] / σd ) · √252

VaR Backtesting

Coverage is validated regulator-style. Kupiec POF tests whether the breach rate matches (1−α); Christoffersen adds an independence test for breach clustering:

LRPOF , LRind , LRcc ~ χ²

An Expected-Shortfall test compares realized tail losses to predicted CVaR.

Filtered vs Smoothed Regimes

The current regime uses filtered probabilities P(st | x1:t) from the forward algorithm — using only information available at time t — rather than the smoothed full-sample posterior, avoiding hindsight bias.

Risk Attribution

Euler decomposition splits total portfolio volatility into per-asset component contributions (which sum to the total), exposing concentration the capital weights hide:

CTRi = wi · (Σw)i / σp

Risk Parity (ERC)

An equal-risk-contribution weighting solved by fixed-point iteration, where every asset contributes the same share of total risk:

wi · (Σw)i = wj · (Σw)j  ∀ i,j

Historical Replay & Reverse Stress

Beyond static shocks, crises are replayed using the actual asset return paths over each window (capturing real contagion). Reverse stress solves for the move required to hit a target loss:

σ-move = Ltarget / σdaily
